Search-snippet-based: direct fetches of youtube.com, eur-lex.europa.eu and ai.google.dev failed (hosts outside the session's allowed network), so primary legal/doc texts were NOT read verbatim; claims below rest on search-result summaries and secondary commentary and are flagged accordingly.

## 1. Automatic prompt optimization and eval-driven iteration; overfitting with tiny datasets

### Takeaway
GEPA (reflective, Pareto-based prompt evolution) and DSPy MIPROv2 are the main practical optimizers; both need a metric and a train/val split. With only a handful of annotated clips, reported evidence shows reflective optimizers can overfit badly, so use a frozen test set, clip-level (match-level) splits, early stopping and regression-gating of any accepted prompt.

### Cited Findings
- GEPA (Genetic-Pareto) reads trajectories, reflects in natural language and keeps a Pareto frontier of candidate prompts; ICLR 2026 oral. v2 reports ~6-point average gain over GRPO (up to 19) with up to 35x fewer rollouts; v1 reported ~+10% average. Also claims >10 points over MIPROv2 on specific benchmarks/models (Qwen3 8B, GPT-4.1 mini), so not universal. — [arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/0e9e708b6f48e14fd0ac29e167413f76-Abstract-Conference.html); code [github.com/gepa-ai/gepa](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa)
- MIPROv2 jointly tunes instructions and few-shot demos (also 0-shot instruction-only mode), proposes candidates and searches combos with Bayesian optimization; DSPy presets "light/medium/heavy". Paper (Opsahl-Ong et al.) reports beating baselines on 5 of 7 multi-stage programs with Llama-3-8B, gains up to 13%; demos often drive biggest gains, instructions matter more for conditional-rule tasks. — [arXiv 2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11695); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11695v2); [DSPy docs](https://dspy.ai/api/optimizers/MIPROv2/)
- OPRO ("Large Language Models as Optimizers"): LLM proposes new prompts from scored history. The paper found training accuracy often 5-20 points above test accuracy; suggested more training data and early stopping. — [arXiv 2309.03409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03409) (as characterized by a search summary; paper body not read)
- TextGrad: natural-language critiques act as "gradients" to revise prompts/solutions (June 2024). Only secondary descriptions found. — [APO survey, arXiv 2502.16923](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16923v1)
- Surveys frame APO formally as: task model + initial prompt + metric + eval set, choose the best prompt by held-out validation score. — [arXiv 2502.16923](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16923v1); [arXiv 2502.18746](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18746v2)
- Small-data overfitting evidence (indicative, mostly non-peer-reviewed): Decagon reports 20-100 training samples worked best for GEPA, 500 worst (more bloat, 10x cost), evaluated on a fixed holdout. — [Decagon](https://decagon.ai/blog/optimizing-gepa-for-production). A MAGE paper with 30 training examples reports GEPA prompts at 34.0% vs 62.4% for the unoptimized seed. — [arXiv 2607.11944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11944). Adaptive overfitting: repeatedly scoring on the same small set and keeping the best inflates scores. — [arXiv 2610.26020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26020). Simon Willison's Datasette Agent experiment (~20 train questions): treat an optimized prompt like a code diff, regression-test before adoption. — [letsdatascience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/dspy-evaluates-and-refines-datasette-agent-prompts-6ced2073). Another paper found test performance peaked at steps ~4-7 then degraded (supports early stopping). — [arXiv 2603.13791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13791). Topic-disjoint train/val splits plus separate held-out test, multi-seed protocol. — [arXiv 2610.12601](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12601)
- DSPy tutorial note: large `max_full_evals` may overspecialize when the validation set is small. — via [arXiv-adjacent search summary](https://huggingface.co/learn/cookbook/en/dspy_gepa)
- Anthropic eval guidance: define specific measurable success criteria, multidimensional; build test sets matching the real task mix including edge cases; can use Claude to expand a baseline set; grading ranking: human (best, slow) vs LLM-graded (scalable, verify reliability first); run multiple trials because outputs vary. — [Anthropic: define success criteria and build evaluations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests); [Anthropic: demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- Google-specific eval-driven guidance: not retrieved (ai.google.dev fetch blocked).

### Inferences
- Recommended protocol for few clips: (a) split by match/player/surface, not by window within a clip (windows of the same clip leak); (b) freeze a test set (e.g. 20-30% of matches) never shown to the optimizer or to you while iterating; (c) leave-one-match-out or k-fold across matches for the optimizer's validation signal; (d) cap optimizer iterations (early stop by validation plateau), use multiple seeds, and require a candidate to beat the incumbent by more than seed-noise on val before promotion; (e) final one-shot test-set score only at release; (f) keep a "regression canary" set of previously-solved hard clips.
- Because GEPA's reflection uses textual feedback, feed it structured diffs (which point/shot is wrong and why) rather than only a scalar; but with ~10s of examples keep prompts short and watch for rule-bloat that memorizes clip specifics (player names, court colour).
- Prefer deterministic code fixes (validators, state machine, OCR) over prompt tweaks for systematic errors; reserve prompt optimization for residual perception errors.

## 2. Metrics for event-sequence extraction and structured-output constraints

### Takeaway
No canonical tennis benchmark for shot-level F1 with tolerance was found, but precise-event-spotting work gives the template: F1 with class match plus temporal tolerance. For LLM-extracted point/shot sequences, combine point-level accuracy, tolerance-matched shot F1, sequence edit distance and hard score-state consistency checks.

### Cited Findings
- Mean F1 with temporal tolerance: a prediction is correct if class matches and timestamp falls within delta frames of ground truth; strict delta=1 frame in precise-spotting work (F3Set-Tennis subset). — [arXiv 2604.22839](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22839)
- Tennis/FineGym/FineDiving/F3Set benchmarks use mAP/F1 at delta in {0,1,2} frames (protocol of Hong et al. 2022). — [arXiv 2602.22073](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22073); precise spotting tolerances ~1-5 frames — [NSF PAR 10403489](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10403489)
- Broadcast tennis event detection paper uses a wider 0.15 s tolerance and reports sweeps from 0 to 0.5 s. — [arXiv 2610.38042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38042)
- Existing tennis datasets: TenniSet (Faulkner and Dick 2017, broadcast footage) and THETIS (1,980 videos, shot types) — cited within the [arXiv 2610.38042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38042) search summary. Their licensing was not checked.

### Inferences
- Because Gemini at ~1 fps (see section 3) cannot localize to 1-2 frames, use coarse tolerances (0.5-1.0 s for shots, 1-2 s for point boundaries) and report a tolerance sweep.
- Suggested metric stack: (1) point-level: exact match of point winner and score-after-point per point; (2) point segmentation F1 (start/end within tolerance); (3) shot-level F1 on (type, player, time within tolerance) via Hungarian/greedy matching; (4) sequence alignment: Levenshtein/Needleman-Wunsch edit distance on shot-type sequences per rally, and rally-length error; (5) score-state consistency: fraction of outputs that are legal tennis score transitions (0/15/30/40/deuce/advantage, tiebreak, set, server rotation, serve-side alternation) and fraction of points where winner assignment is consistent with the next score.
- Constraint techniques: a deterministic tennis score state machine as validator/repair; reject-and-retry with the violation message (two-pass verification: pass 2 receives the pass-1 sequence plus the validator's errors and visible scoreboard frames); JSON schema/enums for structured output. Score-state violations are also a free, label-free signal usable for unsupervised self-improvement.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source on edit-distance or state-machine validation specifically for LLM tennis extraction found; the above is design inference.

## 3. Video-specific design: windowing, OCR anchoring, fps, ensembles, cost

### Takeaway
Gemini samples video at 1 fps by default with a per-frame token budget set by media_resolution; context caching (90% discount on cached input) and Batch (50% discount, up to 24 h) are the main cost levers, though discounts may not stack.

### Cited Findings
- Default video processing extracts frames at 1 FPS; an agentic video mode in newer Flash models adjusts sampling dynamically. — [Gemini video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/video-understanding?authuser=8) (via search summary)
- media_resolution controls tokens per frame; for Gemini 3 video, ~70 tokens/frame at default/low/medium and 280 at high; audio 25 tokens/s. Pages disagree on model naming; treat the official page as authoritative. — [Media resolution](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/media-resolution)
- Batch mode: 50% lower price, results within 24 h. — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/scale-your-ai-workloads-batch-mode-gemini-api/)
- Context caching: 90% discount on cached tokens plus storage; implicit caching enabled by default on Gemini 2.5+; explicit caches with TTL. Vertex docs say cache and batch discounts do not stack and batch does not support explicit caching (Vertex-specific; unconfirmed for the Gemini API). Storage price listed as changing on 1 Jan 2027. — [Gemini API optimization](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/optimization); [Vertex batch docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/multimodal/batch-prediction-gemini)

### Inferences
- Windowing: 30-90 s windows with 5-10 s overlap, deduplicating events in overlaps by tolerance matching; segment into points first (cheap pass: serve/rally/dead-time detection), then extract shots per point clip at higher fps (escalate to 2-5 fps via custom sampling/pre-trimmed clips only for low-confidence points).
- Scoreboard OCR (broadcast overlay) on sampled frames anchors score state at each point boundary, constraining the sequence via the state machine; where no overlay exists (own recordings), track score by manual input or audio announcements.
- Ensembles: sample N=3-5 at temperature >0 and majority-vote per point/shot, using disagreement as escalation trigger; spend extra cost only where votes split.
- Cost: put the long, stable system prompt/few-shots first to exploit caching; use Batch for offline evaluation/optimization runs (optimizer loops are the main spend), real-time path for production. Verify current pricing before budgeting.

- Did not read the official video-understanding page directly (blocked), so fps-customization parameters, max video lengths and token-per-second figures are unverified. Model names in snippets (e.g. "Gemini 3.x Flash") not verified against a model list.

## 4. Legal/ToS risk (research, not legal advice; Spain/EU lens)

### Takeaway
Downloading YouTube broadcast footage with yt-dlp for evaluation breaches YouTube's ToS (contract risk, account/IP blocking) and the footage is third-party copyrighted/broadcaster-controlled; the EU Art. 4 TDM exception may cover internal analysis but is opt-out-able and unclear for audiovisual/commercial product use. Sackmann data is non-commercial. Safest path: own recordings, permissioned academy footage, or licensed data.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube says its ToS prohibits unauthorized downloading and scraping; its CEO called third-party scraping for AI training a "clear violation" (2024). Creators may opt in to third-party AI training. Whether YouTube's license covers AI training is undecided in court. — [YouTube Help: content and third-party training](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15509945); [Fortune 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/04/04/openai-youtube-clear-violation-terms-service-ai-sora-training/)
- ToS text (via archived 2016/2017 versions and a university FAQ, not current text): no downloading/reproducing unless the service offers a download link or YouTube gives written permission; no circumventing security/copy-restriction features; use gives no IP rights. — [archived ToS 2017](https://web-wp.archive.org/web/20170811164130/https:/www.youtube.com/t/terms); [Univ. Waterloo FAQ](https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright-at-waterloo/faq-0-9). Current wording should be checked at [youtube.com/t/terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms).
- yt-dlp/youtube-dl legality: RIAA 2020 DMCA 1201 claim against youtube-dl; EFF argued no circumvention of DRM occurs; GitHub reinstated and set up a $1M defense fund. This addresses US tool-distribution law, not whether using it breaches ToS or copyright. — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2020/11/github-reinstates-youtube-dl-after-riaas-abuse-dmca); [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/standing-up-for-developers-youtube-dl-is-back/). EFF notes most countries adopted similar anti-circumvention rules; no source found on German/Spanish court treatment (a Hamburg ruling query returned nothing).
- EU DSM Directive 2019/790: Art. 3 = TDM for scientific research by research organisations/cultural heritage institutions with lawful access, cannot be overridden by contract; Art. 4 = TDM by anyone with lawful access, including commercial, but rightholders may reserve rights (machine-readable opt-out, e.g. robots.txt-type); copies kept only as long as necessary. — [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-new-copyright-directive-text-and-data-mining-articles-3-and-4/); [Stanford CIS](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/publications/text-and-data-mining-articles-3-and-4-directive-2019790eu/). Official EUR-Lex text not read.
- Spain: Art. 67 of Real Decreto-ley 24/2021 transposes Arts. 3-4; commentators (Communia/EARE) say the transposition conflates/imperfectly separates research and commercial TDM and that the research exception apparently allows rightholder opt-out contrary to the Directive. Spain also floated an extended collective licensing royal decree for AI training (2024 proposal; current status not verified). — [Kluwer: first look at Spanish ECL proposal](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/a-first-look-at-the-spanish-proposal-to-introduce-ecl-for-ai-training/); [EARE position](https://eare.eu/eare-position-on-the-spanish-royal-decree-to-introduce-extended-collective-licenses-for-ai-training/); [CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/esp/publication/royal-decree-law-24-2021-of-2-november)
- A 2025 Hamburg Higher Regional Court decision is reported as strengthening the technical (machine-readable) opt-out model; details not verified. — [Ecija summary](https://www.ecija.com/en/news-and-insights/alemania-el-tribunal-superior-de-hamburgo-refuerza-el-modelo-europeo-de-opt-out-tecnico-en-mineria-de-datos/)
- Sackmann data (tennis_atp/wla): licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 per multiple mirrors (attribution, non-commercial, share-alike); one unverified third-party claim says the GitHub repos returned 404 on 2 Nov 2026. License text itself not fetched from creativecommons.org/GitHub. Recommends contacting Sackmann for commercial permission. — [Tennis Abstract blog](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2015/03/24/free-atp-and-wta-results-and-stats-databases/); mirrors e.g. [Tennismylife/TML-Database](https://github.com/Tennismylife/TML-Database)
- ATP official data is commercially licensed via Sportradar (an "Official ATP addendum" exists), indicating official stats/data are rights-managed. — [Sportradar ATP addendum](https://sportradar.com/official-atp-addendum/?lang=en-us)

### Inferences
- Contract (ToS) risk is independent of copyright exceptions: even if Art. 4 TDM applies, a ToS bar on downloading can still be enforced against the account/IP; and the Art. 4 exception requires "lawful access" which is arguable when obtaining copies via prohibited means.
- Broadcast footage involves layered rights (broadcaster, tournament organiser, ATP/WTA, music/graphics); Spain has no US-style open-ended fair use, only closed exceptions (quotation, TDM, etc.), so "fair use" arguments are weak in an EU base; an internal, non-distributed, small-scale evaluation set is lower risk than a product that redistributes clips or trains/fine-tunes commercially, but is not risk-free.
- A commercial product using only API-based prompting (no model training) and its own labels is less exposed than one that redistributes footage, but benchmarks/ground truth derived from Sackmann (NC-SA) cannot sit inside a commercial product without permission, and share-alike could propagate to derived datasets.
- Safer alternatives: record own matches/practice (get player consent; GDPR applies to identifiable people incl. minors, image rights under Spanish LO 1/1982); written agreements with academies/clubs; licensed datasets (check each license, e.g. TenniSet/THETIS terms unverified); ask rights-holders or Sportradar/Hawk-Eye/IMG-type providers for evaluation licences; use YouTube only via embedded/URL-based Gemini input where permitted (YouTube URL input in Gemini is not verified here) rather than local downloads, and consider creators who opted in to third-party training.
