## Match Charting Project (MCP): notation, outputs, how volunteers chart

### Takeaway
MCP is a volunteer, Excel-workbook, shot-by-shot charting effort (5,000+ matches per the README). Only the "1st", "2nd" and "Notes" columns are human-entered; everything else (winner/error flags, rally count) is derived from the codes. The full notation table was not retrievable; only partial codes were confirmed via search summary.

### Cited Findings
- Project started late 2013; 5,000+ charted matches from dozens of contributors; men's and women's in separate files — [MCP GitHub README](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)
- Charters use the MatchChart Excel workbook (`MatchChart 0.3.2.xlsm`); its "Instructions" tab holds the full notation — [MCP GitHub README](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)
- README: only "1st", "2nd", "Notes" columns are user-submitted; "All of the other columns are automatically generated by the excel doc." — [MCP GitHub README](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)
- Data dictionary says each point's first/second-serve and rally are coded in 1st/2nd; derived columns are 1stSV, 2ndSV, 1stIn, 2ndIn, isAce, isUnret, isRallyWinner, isForced, isUnforced, isDouble, PtWinner, isSvrWinner, rallyCount; Notes is free text, sometimes challenge outcomes; the dictionary defers codes to the "Instructions" tab — [data_dictionary.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject/master/data_dictionary.txt)
- Point-level fields also include set/game/point score, tiebreak flags, server/returner — [data_dictionary.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject/master/data_dictionary.txt)
- Partial notation (search summary, not full-page verified): serve direction 4 = wide, 5 = body, 6 = down the T; shot letters f = forehand, b = backhand, s/r slices; "@" = unforced error, "#" = forced error, "*" = winner; error-type letters n (net), w (wide), d (deep), x (wide and deep); "+" marks approach shots; example "4ffbbf*" = wide serve, then forehand return, forehand, backhand, backhand, forehand winner — search results citing [Tennis Abstract quick start guide](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2015/09/23/the-match-charting-project-quick-start-guide/) (page itself not read)
- README does not describe any validation/error-checking process for submitted charts — [MCP GitHub README](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)
- Derived stats files (ServeBasics, ReturnDepth, ShotDirection, etc.) are aggregate totals — [MCP GitHub README](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)
- By the 10,000-chart milestone, contributors had logged ~6 million shots and 1.5M+ points (search summary) — [Tennis Abstract: 10,000 Charted Matches](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/?p=5090)

### Inferences
- Because outcome/rally fields are computed from human-typed codes, MCP serves as ground truth for serve direction, shot type/direction, outcome class and rally length; any AI target must produce an equivalent per-shot string.
- Depth codes and exact winner/error-type definitions are unverified here.

### Gaps
- Exact documented definitions of winner/forced/unforced, depth codes, shot-type letters (volleys, lobs, drop shots), direction digits for groundstrokes: not retrieved (Instructions tab / tennisabstract.com blocked).

## Professional tagging (ATP/Infosys, Hawk-Eye, Sportradar, Opta-style)

### Takeaway
Little primary documentation was accessible. Sportradar describes live scout entry with failover and tiered point-by-point coverage; no accuracy or latency figures were found.

### Cited Findings
- Sportradar docs (search summary): if the scout feed fails, Sportradar's Live Data Entry (LDE) team takes over as failover; point-by-point can continue while service outcomes (aces, double faults, faults) are withheld until post-match verification — [Sportradar Tennis docs](https://developer.sportradar.com/tennis/docs/tennis-ig-live-match-retrieval)
- Coverage tiers (search summary): Grand Slams get 100% point-by-point from round 1; some lower-tier women's events only from semifinals; Billie Jean King Cup and some Davis Cup ties have none — [Sportradar coverage tiers](https://developer.sportradar.com/tennis/docs/tennis-ig-data-coverage-tiers)
- Sportradar push toward "true real time" aims to close the gap between tracking tech knowing a result and the umpire entering it; described as a next step, not delivered (search summary) — [SportBusiness](https://www.sportbusiness.com/2026/05/tennis-tech-evolution-delivering-results-on-and-off-the-court/)
- Hawk-Eye SkeleTRACK optical skeletal tracking debuted at Laver Cup 2024; such devices not permitted at a number of tournaments (search summary) — [Hawk-Eye](https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4243365/skeletrack-a-new-era-of-data-in-tennis)

### Inferences
- Scout-entered, umpire-chair-driven data is reliable for score and serve outcomes, with verification after the match; subjective categories are presumably less central to live feeds (not confirmed).

### Gaps
- ATP/Infosys workflow, Opta-style tagger procedures, data field lists, live latency in seconds, and reported accuracy percentages: not found.
- How Hawk-Eye tracking data merges with scout data: not found.

## Inter-annotator agreement and error rates

### Takeaway
No formal kappa or percent-agreement figure for human tennis charters was found. Qualitative evidence shows forced vs unforced error is highly subjective.

### Cited Findings
- A study of 8,800+ MCP matches found charters agree most on patterns that almost always yield one class and disagree most in a "grey zone"; the same shot may be forced for one player and unforced for another (search summary; page unreachable) — [Consistency in Forced vs Unforced Error Classification in the MCP](https://ramizheman.github.io/mcp-charter-consistency). Third-party analysis, not by Sackmann.
- FiveThirtyEight writer's own Wimbledon count found 21% more unforced errors than official tallies; club spokesman: "The labeling of errors as forced or unforced is very subjective so inevitably there will be differences between one score and another." (search summary) — [FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/wimbledon-is-very-forgiving-of-players-errors)
- Craig O'Shannessy: "Whether it's forced or unforced is difficult to judge." (search summary) — [Tennis.com](https://prod.tennis.com/news/articles/you-ve-missed-but-was-it-really-an-unforced-error)
- Simon Fraser paper notes forced/unforced is often subjective and typical datasets lack the distinction — [arXiv 2407.19321](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19321v1)
- Jindo, Mitsuhashi & Kubota compared self-rated stats with video-based counts in 30 collegiate players (not inter-rater); mean differences not significant overall; unforced-error mean difference -2.133 (search summary; year listed inconsistently 2022/2025) — [IJRSS](https://revistaseug.ugr.es/index.php/IJRSS/article/view/33262)

### Inferences
- A realistic AI target for UE vs forced is "agreement with a typical human charter," likely well below 100%; human ceiling is unquantified.

### Gaps
- Any published Cohen's kappa / agreement rate between charters, and any Sackmann statement on MCP data quality: not found.

## Objective vs subjective stats and realistic AI targets

### Takeaway
Score, aces, double faults, point winner, and rally length are determined by events (verifiable); UE vs forced, shot type, and placement involve judgment.

### Cited Findings
- MCP derives isAce, isDouble, PtWinner, rallyCount mechanically from codes, while isForced/isUnforced come from a charter's "@"/"#" choice — [data_dictionary.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject/master/data_dictionary.txt)
- Sportradar withholds service outcomes (aces/faults) until post-match verification when scouts drop out, implying they are verifiable but need confirmation — [Sportradar docs](https://developer.sportradar.com/tennis/docs/tennis-ig-live-match-retrieval)
- Forced/unforced is called subjective by Wimbledon's own spokesman — [FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/wimbledon-is-very-forgiving-of-players-errors)

### Inferences
- Objective tier (high-accuracy target): score, point winner, ace/double fault, rally length, serve direction. Subjective tier (report with agreement caveats): UE vs forced, shot type, depth/placement.
- Serve speed is radar-measured, not tagged; no source read here.

### Gaps
- Per-stat human error rates; serve speed provenance.
